# Scale BB-D — supplementary material and reproduction package

Supplementary material for the ICA 2026 presentation of:

> *Extending Scale BB from All-Cause to Cause-Specific Mortality: A Scenario
> Generator for Disease-Contingent Insurance under Economic-Value-Based
> Valuation (ICS/IFRS 17)*

**The paper itself is distributed officially by the ICA 2026 organisers and is
not reproduced here.** This repository is the additional material for those who
attended the presentation: the code, the input data, and the full numerical
results behind the findings, so that anything shown on a slide can be traced,
checked and re-run.

**Scale BB-D** extends Scale BB — the mortality improvement scale published by
the Society of Actuaries in 2012 — from all-cause to cause-specific
(disease-specific) mortality, and repositions it as a **scenario generator** for
economic-value-based valuation frameworks (ICS, IFRS 17, and Japan's ESR).

## Headline results

The paper has a deliberate three-step structure. The numbers below are the ones
regenerated by the code in this repository (`sex = total`).

**1. Negative result (§5).** As a *point forecast* of cause-specific mortality,
Scale BB-D loses to the best simple baseline on MAPE in every cell — 8 diseases
× 3 sexes × 3 training cutoffs. Scale BB-D MAPE [%]:

| disease | cutoff 2014 | cutoff 2021 | cutoff 2022 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| cancer | 22.41 | 9.20 | 8.87 |
| cerebrovascular | 47.14 | 15.07 | 15.52 |
| diabetes | 35.59 | 16.02 | 13.29 |
| heart_disease | 31.27 | 10.27 | 8.37 |
| hypertensive | 73.83 | 24.13 | 20.53 |
| kidney | 40.87 | 16.69 | 15.59 |
| liver | 49.49 | 29.17 | 29.52 |
| total | 26.01 | 9.33 | 7.33 |

With cutoff 2014 the gap against the best baseline is +16 to +44pp.

**2. Unexpected discovery (§6).** Measured by the *direction* of change, the
same model output reaches 77.7–95.0% directional accuracy for the six main
diseases. Directional accuracy DA [%] at cutoff 2014:

| disease | Scale BB-D | naive_last | loglin_trend |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| total | 95.00 | 0.00 | 94.29 |
| cerebrovascular | 91.04 | 0.00 | 91.04 |
| cancer | 79.71 | 0.00 | 93.48 |

`naive_last` scores 0.00 everywhere by construction: its predicted change is
identically zero, so it carries no direction information at all.

**3. Repositioning (§7–§9).** Economic-value-based valuation asks for
internally consistent scenarios rather than point accuracy, so the model is
repositioned as a scenario generator. The BEL sensitivity demonstration under
Japan's ESR shows an age structure of trend risk — up to about 1.7× the uniform
regulatory shock at young entry ages — that current uniform-factor practice
cannot capture.

## Reproducing the backtest (§5–§6)

Requires Python 3.10+ with `pandas`, `numpy`, `scipy`, `matplotlib`.

```bash
cd reproduction/backtest
bash run_all.sh          # or: PY=/path/to/python bash run_all.sh
```

This runs in a few minutes and regenerates every table and figure of §5–§6
under `reproduction/backtest/output/`, starting from the raw vital-statistics
table. `reproduction/backtest/README.md` lists reference values to check your
run against.

The forward-looking pipeline that produces generational assumed-rate tables
(§3.3) is documented separately in `reproduction/generational/README.md`.

## Data

The single primary input is the cause-specific mortality table of the **Vital
Statistics of Japan** (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare / e-Stat,
1950–2024, 8 diseases × 3 sexes × 21 age groups), bundled unmodified together
with the derived analysis panels. See `DATA_SOURCES.md` for citations and terms
of use.
